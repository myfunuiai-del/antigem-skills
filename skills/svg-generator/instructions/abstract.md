# Abstract tracing hints

- There is no single concrete subject to silhouette — favor several smaller `class="fill"` regions over one dominant outline, distributed to match the source's actual shape/color layout.
- Author every `class="fill"`/`class="stroke-sil"`/`class="stroke-int"` shape as a curved `<path>` (with at least one `Q`/`C` command), `<circle>`, or `<ellipse>` — never a `<polygon>` or `<rect>`, per SKILL.md's **Line rules**. Even geometric forms (triangles, bars, angular blocks) must be traced as gently curved `<path>`s, not polygons — the wobbly hand-drawn edge is mandatory.
- If the source is meme/text-heavy, prioritize tracing the dominant graphic element (face, icon, or shape) the text sits on rather than attempting to render the text itself — this style has no text-rendering rules.
- Use `class="stroke-int"` more than `class="stroke-sil"`: abstract compositions usually read as a cluster of interior boundaries rather than one outer silhouette. Class boundaries normally regardless of size — canvas.py scales the wobble/width floor down proportionately for small shapes (see SKILL.md), so there's no need to leave small interior boundaries unclassed.
- Lean on the palette's contrast — alternate adjacent fill regions between visually distinct palette colors so regions stay legible without relying on outline alone.
- Target 8-12 `class="fill"` regions for a typical abstract composition, matching the density used for portraits and objects — this is well within SKILL.md's fill-region and line ceilings (there is no separate, lower budget for this category). Simplify busy source compositions down to their most visually dominant shapes, but don't under-fill: a handful of large flat regions reads as sparse against approved targets.
- **Named failure mode — do not do this:** tracing 2-3 large flat fills and treating that as "abstract simplicity." Abstract sources are usually busier in color/shape variation than portraits or objects — favor more, smaller fill regions with varied palette colors over fewer, larger ones.
