# Object tracing hints

- Split subjects with distinct structural parts (head, torso, limbs, tail, appendages) into one fill region per part — do not use a single silhouette fill blob for the whole subject. Each part is its own closed shape with its own outline, even when every part shares the same color.
- Author every `class="fill"`/`class="stroke-sil"`/`class="stroke-int"` shape as a curved `<path>` (with at least one `Q`/`C` command), `<circle>`, or `<ellipse>` — never a `<polygon>` or `<rect>`, per SKILL.md's **Line rules**. Even geometric objects with straight real-world edges (boxes, labels, mechanical panels, signage) must be traced as gently curved `<path>`s, not polygons.
- Each distinct body part gets its own `class="fill"` region. Where two parts meet (e.g. arm overlapping torso, head meeting neck), the boundary between them is a `class="stroke-int"` line regardless of the part's size — canvas.py scales the wobble/width floor down proportionately for small shapes (see SKILL.md).
- Background or environment zones (ground, furniture, props the subject sits on/against) get their own separate `class="fill"` regions if they're a distinct color from the subject — do not merge them into the subject's silhouette or leave them as bare background.
- Trace a tight outer silhouette per part: each part's `stroke-sil` path hugs that part's actual contour — avoid loose/rounded approximations that lose the subject's distinguishing shape, and avoid one bounding silhouette that swallows multiple parts.
- **Named failure mode — do not do this:** authoring the subject as a single large silhouette fill with interior lines drawn for limbs. Interior lines on top of one big fill still render as one fill region with surface texture; they do not separate the limb. Limbs must be separate fill regions with their own contour paths, not interior strokes on a shared fill.
- Interior detail lines (seams, labels, mechanical parts) on a single-part object get `stroke-int`; keep these to the object's structurally meaningful edges, not surface texture.
- Use `class="fill"` for each part's dominant material color, including small accent details (logos, highlights) — canvas.py scales the scribble fill down proportionately for small shapes (see SKILL.md), so class them normally rather than leaving them unclassed.
- Center the object in the usable zone with generous, even margins — objects read best isolated rather than cropped tight to the frame edge.
